## Everywhere

- Aim for purposeful minimalism: modern, highly readable, never a templated look. Every element on a screen has a reason; no helper lines, captions or filler labels the reader does not need to act.
- Simple experience, complex engine: the person sees few choices and few steps between screens, and the work hides behind them.
- One word per label. No slash-joined alternatives, no needless quotes or brackets, and no bare codes such as card or question numbers on anything a person reads.
- Write so a 13-year-old can follow it. Specialist terms keep their names and are explained where they first appear.
- Real contrast and clear highlights: primary, secondary and quiet buttons look different, the way well-known software does. One professional icon set, used the same way everywhere.
- One fixed layout frame: switching tabs changes only what is inside the boxes, never their size or place.
- Check layouts at the real screen sizes the user names, with layout rules written as numbers, never judged by eye at one size.
- Earlier approved visuals are the approved style. A build lifts and refines them, never invents new ones or downgrades them.
- A diagram called a timeline reads left to right at a glance.

## Sessions

- A comment on a review page is not agreement. Rework what was commented on and show it again until the user says they agree; an answered page is not a go.
- Put a strong recommendation on every decision card, so a short answer is enough.
- Leaving things out reads as losing work. A synthesis folds in every research, trial and lesson, and conflicts are reconciled and shown, not dropped.
- Findings pages show the real evidence (screens, how deep each source was read, numbers), not a few merged cards.
- Requirements come first: every element names its requirement, then the research behind it. Each stage output lists the research nothing used yet, and research that serves no requirement is parked.
- Before calling anything done, open a few real items and check them. Counts are not proof.
- Dates and commitments belong to the user. Flag a date that has passed; never change it.
